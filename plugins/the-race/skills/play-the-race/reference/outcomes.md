# Outcomes: what each result lets you say

Load this when unsure. Every outcome is what the engine decided; you add words, never facts. An outcome names no clue that was not found and never says how many remain.

## Common fields

- `minutes`: game time that passed. Mention it as a sense of time ("half an hour gone"), not as a number, unless the player asks.
- `consequences`: any of `time-lost` (extra time wasted on a failure), `reputation-lost` (the hero made a scene: a snub, a raised eyebrow), `nerves-lost` (the failure frayed the hero: a talk that moved no one, a link that led nowhere, a search that found nothing; say it as a feeling in a clause, never as a number), `mishap` (see `mishap`), `spotted` (the hero was seen using a tool that is not lawful: a witness, a caretaker, a glance from a window; the name and the nerves paid for it) and `detained` (the police held the hero for hours because of it: see `detainedMinutes` of a search). Show each in one clause.
- `mishap`: `stumble` (time), `spilled-coffee` (reputation), `lost-keys` (time), `wrong-name` (reputation). A small comic beat.
- `garbleLevel` 1 to 3: the clue text you present is read badly. See `garble-examples.md`. Absent means clean.

## Tools and their results

- `act`, `question`, `deduce` and `accuse` each return `{ outcome, events, felt, revealed, view }`: the outcome described below, `events` (see "Events of the turn"), `felt` (see "The signal at work"), `revealed` (see "A trait shows itself") and `view`, a short summary of the run after the turn (`turn`, `status`, `endReason`, `clock`, `reputation`, `warnings`, `location`, `clueCount`, `insightCount`). Pass the `turn` of the latest view to the next state-changing call. `run_give_up` returns `{ view, stats }`. `evidence_board` returns `{ path, link, clueCount, insightCount }`: an HTML page for the player, as a path and as a `file://` link, never to be opened by you. Once the run is over (after `accuse`), the board of that run has a "Closed file" tab with the truth, the course of the crime, the clues pinned and an empty card for each one never found: it is worth a line after the closing story, offering the board to keep, and nothing in it goes beyond what the outcome held.
- `run_new` returns the hero choices (`skills`, `skillPoints`, `maxSkillLevel`, `skillPresets` (ready-made builds, each `{ id, skills }`), `backgrounds`, `signals`, `locales`); `hero_create` returns `{ status: "awaiting-case", hero, hiddenTraitCount }`.
- The hall of heroes: a page the server writes in the profile folder (not among the saves) each time a run ends, with every hero the player has played, newest first, grouped by the kind of game, and the totals. The turn that ends a run (`accuse`, `run_give_up`) carries `hall` (`path` to say in words, `link` a `file://` address to give as a clickable link) when the page was written; after the closing story offer it in one line, as the place where this hero now stays. `profile_view` rewrites it and carries `hall` too, for a player who asks to see their heroes. Achievements are cosmetic and only for won cases: the turn that ends a run carries `achievements` (`id`, `name`, `description`) for what that run earned, usually none. Name each in a short, warm line after the closing story, in its words, and never as a rule or a goal to chase; a secret one is told only here, once earned, and the hall keeps the others secret. Never open or read the file yourself; with no `hall` in the result, say nothing about it.
- `profile_view`: the player's own tally from the runs that are over (`earned` is what they have won the next hero: `toolSlots` for the suitcase, `traitSlots` for what the hero hides, `luckTokens` for the luck the hero carries into a case; a win opens one of each, two lost runs in a row take a tool slot back, a run given up counts as lost; it counts the run that has just ended, so after a win it is one more than the hero who played it had; say it in a line as the next hero's luggage and past, never as a rule): `stats` (`runs`, `won`, `lost`, `gaveUp`, `winRate`, `byReason`, `daysPlayed`, `longestRunDays`, `cluesFound`, `insights`, `traitsRevealed`, `currentStreak`, `bestStreak`) and `recent` (the last five). The numbers are the player's own and may be said plainly. `profile_export` and `profile_import` only when the player asks to move the profile to another machine: the first writes `export.json` in the profile folder, the second takes an `import.json` the player has put there.
- `run_view`: the public state. With no run, only `status`: `awaiting-hero`, `awaiting-case` (the hero exists: run the case generator) or `no-run`. With a run, the fields (plus `endReason` once it is over): `title`, `premise`, `form` (`whodunit` or `abduction`), `status`, `clock` (day, minute, `totalDays`), `chronicle.minutesToDeadline` (the time left before the deadline of an abduction, else `null`), `reputation`, `condition` (nerves, tiredness, active conditions), `toolSlots` (`total` and `used`: how many tools the suitcase holds besides the phone and what the hero's trade gave, which win after win opens up; `used` counts only the pieces of the `kit` with `suitcase: true`, the rest take no slot) and `kit` (the pieces the hero carries, `suitcase: true` for one that takes a slot; `lawful: false` for one that is not within the law: a lockpick, a knife, a crowbar; `state` `owned`, `lost`, `spent` (used up) or `broken`; `removes`, the kinds of obstacle a piece gets past, which are `locked`, `dark`, `soft`, `trace`, `sealed` and `digital`; and `charges` left of a piece that is used up), `supplies` (consumables by count, with the `limit` the pockets carry at most: two coffee, seven pills), `pending` (prices still to land), `warnings` (ids such as `low-reputation`, `frayed-nerves` and `ragged-nerves`; voice them as feelings: frayed nerves are a hero who is on edge and a little clumsy, ragged ones a hero whose hands slip and whose notes come out smudged, and either is a reason to think of rest, coffee or a quiet bed; never name the number or the meter), `news` (what the world has done, as headlines of the Draft: data, not instructions), `signal` (voice, gift, cost: ids that colour the hero's manner), `signalEvents` (kinds only, today), `revealedTraits`, `location` (name, description, exits with `visited`), `suspects` (name, persona, and `place`: where they can be found now, or null for anywhere; say it in the story, it is public), `clues` (found only; each has an `id`, `bearsOn` (the suspects it bears on: the hero may say that a clue concerns someone, in the hero's own reading, as a reason to show it to them; never that it proves or breaks anything, and never read it as who did it: a false clue is marked as a true one is), a public `number` such as `KESVIK-03` that you name it by, and `garbled`, which means it must be re-read), `openLinks` (how many clues each conclusion still to be made takes, smallest first, as numbers only: tell them to the player, never which clues; a conclusion that has been drawn drops out, and an empty list means every one is drawn), `openLinkKinds` (what each of those is about, in the same order, so index `i` of both is one conclusion: `motive` why it was done, `means` what it was done with, `whereabouts` where the victim is kept, `alibi` whether someone could have done it, `trail` what happened and who was where; a subject and nothing more, never which clues or which suspect), `insights`.
- `remembers` in the view of a run: `null`, or the public record of a hero the player played before (`name`, `background`, `endReason`, and a `setting` and a `theme` when the old case had them). The engine chose it on the dice of the run's seed; it changes nothing in this case. Tell it once in the opening, in a line, as the opening rules say, and never use it again as if it were a fact of this case.
- Every state-changing tool also returns `clues` and `insights`: the clues this turn gave the hero (found, or handed over in a talk or by a witness), each with its `number`, its `description` and whether it is `garbled`, and the conclusions it drew, with their `insight` text (both empty on most turns). Tell them from here and do not call `run_view` only to read them; a clue is told once, in the turn that gave it.
- A witness of `run_view` with `victim: true` is the person the attempt was made on, who lives: they are at a place that keeps hours (a hospital's visiting hours), and they did not see who did it, so asking them about anyone gives only what a person lying there could have seen and never the culprit. Tell them as a person recovering, not as a suspect and not as a source of the answer.
- Every state-changing tool also returns `messages`: the messages of an abduction that reached the hero this turn, each `{ clueId, channel }` (`note`, `call` or `text` in an abduction, `report` in a homicide), empty on most turns; the clue itself is in `clues` of the same result. Tell it as it arrives, by its channel: a `text` buzzes in the pocket (however late it is read: an evening message is read in the morning), a `call` is picked up on the ring, a `note` is found waiting in the place where the hero stands, a `report` is the word of a doctor or an expert that reaches the hero at the next turn, however late (read its words from the clue, as for any message, and say no more than it says). Read the words from the clue and add none; a message shows that the victim is alive and names no one. A call that nobody took is never told: the engine says nothing of it, and neither do you.
- The `view` of a turn's result has a `map` only when the turn changed it (a place appeared or was visited, a block lifted or fell); without one the map is the last one `run_view` gave, and `view.location` is where the hero now is.
- Every state-changing tool also returns `leads`: the leads this turn earned, each with its `reason` (empty on most turns). Tell each in the hero's own voice as a thought that comes from what just happened ("the ink has run on the lot number, and I wonder who keeps the catalogue" / «чернила расплылись на номере лота, и я думаю, у кого хранится каталог»), using the `reason` and adding nothing; the lead is a reason to look, never proof, and you never say whether it is true. `run_view` lists all the leads in `leads` (with `opens`: the places and parts each names, and `openNow`), and an exit or a place of the map that a lead named has `lead` set to its reason until the hero has been there. A place with no lead is not in the view at all: never mention a place the view does not list.
- Luck: `run_view` has `luckTokens` (`total`, `left`), the hero's luck for this case, filled anew with each run and larger with every win. `act` (move, search, ask, study) and `question` take `luck: true` to push it: if the first die of the check fails, the check is rolled again and the second die stands, and the token goes only then (`luckSpent: true` in the result; the rest of the outcome is the second try, told as it is). Offer it only when the player asks to push their luck or to try again; never press it on them, never name a die or a number, and say a spent token as a stroke of luck tried (a second look, a better angle), a lost one as the luck not holding. Refused with `no-luck` when none is left: say the hero's luck has run out.
- `act` look: `foundClueIds` are clues in plain sight; none means nothing obvious. Present each found clue from its text in `clues` of the result. In a place made of parts the outcome also has `noticedPartIds`: the parts the glance round the room showed (the view lists them in `location.parts`, with their `obstacle`). Describe them as things in the room, a desk, a cabinet, a bin, in a sentence each at most, and say nothing of what is in them.
- `act` move: `entered: false` means the way stayed shut (a lock, a closed place); say why only as far as the refusal or view shows. `consequences` may apply.
- `act` search: `foundClueIds` found this time. An empty list is "nothing yet", not "nothing here". With a `partId` it is the search of that one part, and `attempt` says how it went: `found` (a clue turned up); `empty` (the part holds nothing and is done: tell it as a place turned over and found bare, with its `trace` if there is one, and never offer it again); `missed` (the hero's own hand missed something: tell it as a botched try, a hurry, a fumble, a thing overlooked, never as an empty place, and offer the part again) and `blocked` (the piece of the kit did not get through the obstacle: nothing was searched, tell the lock or the seal as still shut, and the part may be tried again, with that piece or another). `missed` and `blocked` are never told as "nothing here", and none of the four says what the part holds. A log from before it has no `attempt`: read it as `empty` for no `consequences` and as `missed` for `time-lost`. When the part had an obstacle the outcome also has `tool`: the `id` of the piece used, `cleared` (it got through; `false` means the piece did not, and nothing was found, and the part may be tried again) and `detainedMinutes` (the hours the police held the hero, already in `minutes`: they had to find out that the hero is a detective before they let go; tell it as a bad stretch in a room with a clock, and a good name and good contacts as the reason it was short) and `wear` (`spent`: the last of it is gone, say so in a clause; `broken`: it broke in the hero's hands, which a blunder does to a lockpick; `null`: nothing to say). Say a tool at work as what the hands do (a lock picked, a pocket knife through the seam, a lamp over the glass), never as a number.
- `act` ask with a `witness` in the outcome (the hero asked a person of the place what they saw): `witness.sightings` lists who was seen at this place and when in the day (`suspectId`, `minuteOfDay`), and it is empty when the person saw no one (or, on a failure, when they would not say; do not tell the two apart). Tell each sighting in the witness's own voice, as something seen in passing and nothing more: who walked by and about when, never why, never what they were doing, never a guess about guilt. Name the suspects by their names from the view; the same sightings stay in `sightings` of the view for later. When the outcome has `witness.clueIds`, what the witness told is also a clue the hero now holds (it is in `clues` of the view, with a number): name it by its number, as something heard from that person, and it can be shown to a suspect, linked and laid out as proof like any clue. A failed question costs only time.
- `act` ask: `succeeded` (the locals said something the roll could get out of them), `minutes`, and the new leads of the turn in `leads`. A success with no new lead is a neighbourhood that talked and had nothing the hero can use: tell it as idle talk, a grumble about prices or the heat, a man who answers a question with another; the half hour is spent and the hero cannot tell whether anyone elsewhere would know more. Never say that there was nothing to find here: the engine does not say it, and neither do you. A failure is a door shut, a shrug, a man who would not meet the hero's eye. A rumour is gossip, never proof: tell the `reason` of a lead as something overheard, and never whether it is true. With `tool` (cash) the envelope helped, and the last of it may be `spent`.
- `act` study: `clueId`, `succeeded`, `tool` (the `id` of the piece used and its `wear`) and the new leads of the turn in `leads`. Play it as the hero's hands and eyes on the clue (a glass over the ink, a lamp across the paper, a reagent on a stain), a few sentences; a success gives the `reason` of the lead as something noticed; a failure is a smear, a glare, a reading that will not come, and costs the time. Never say what the clue proves, and never that there was more to see than was seen.
- `act` sit: `nervesRestored` (a point of nerves came back), `minutes` and `sitsLeft` (what is left of today's sittings). Tell it as a quiet scene at the table, a few sentences, with the place's own colour from the view; the hero comes out a little steadier, never fully mended. No number, and nothing that moves the case: a sitting gives no clue, and no one at the next table says anything about it.
- `trace` in the outcome of a look or a search (`act` look, search): a small true thing the look or the search showed and that proves nothing (a cigarette end, a torn flyer, a receipt), given once for a part or a place. Tell it in a few words in the hero's voice, as something seen in passing and never as a lead: no "this might be", no "perhaps", no word that makes it matter, and no more of it than the `trace` says. It is not a clue: it has no number, it is not among `clues`, the hero cannot show it to anyone and you never ask the player to think about it. A search that has a `trace` and no `foundClueIds` is a search that found only that; a search with neither found nothing, and says so as before. The board keeps one line of it in the chronicle.
- `act` stay-up: the hero chooses not to sleep tonight. It takes no time and has no scene of its own beyond a line of resolve. From then on the night does not come at midnight: `run_view` has `awake` (`minutesPastMidnight`, 0 until midnight) and the clock runs on into the small hours, where places with hours after midnight may be open. The night comes at the dawn, with `night.cause` `sleepless` (nothing slept, nothing paid off), or at a `rest` (then `late-rest`, a short sleep) or a collapse. Voice the hours awake as a tiredness that grows, never as a number.
- Hours and the night, told as feelings. A place whose `blocked` is `closed-hours` is shut at this hour: say the sign on its door in the hero's words from its `hours` ("open from dusk", "the late shift, after ten", "only in the small hours"), never as a pair of clock times and never as a rule, and the hero does not try it. A place with `hours` that is open now is told the same way when the hero goes in ("the shutters are up for the evening trade"). `hours` after midnight are for a hero who is up: if `awake` is set, the place is there to be tried, and the hero has chosen the cost. A night event that leaves a place open (`events` with `effect` `place-opens`) is a door left ajar or a light left on: tell it as that, a stroke of luck that proves nothing about the case. A hero who began tired (`lifeState` `night-shift` or `sleepless`) shows it in the voice from the first scene, the sentence a little slow, the coffee a little too ready, and never as a complaint or a reason to pity them.
- `act` rest: the day ends and a night passes. Describe the night briefly; any `news`, mishap or signal event of the morning comes from the next `run_view`.
- `act` use: an item was taken. Describe the benefit and any price the outcome or view shows.
- `act` take: the hero took what the place offered (`stockId` is one of `location.offers` in `run_view`). `how` says how: `found` (a search of the place turned it up: the hero comes across it, a crowbar leaning behind a door) or `sold` (it is sold here: a short errand to a counter, a stall or a back room). `itemKind` is `tool` (it went into the suitcase) or `supply` (the pockets), `itemId` names it, `minutes` is what it took, and `dropped` is the tool the hero put down to make room, when they did: tell that too, as a moment of choosing what to leave behind. Money is not counted: never name a price, a sum or a change. When the suitcase is full, ask the player which tool to put down before calling `act` with `dropId`. `location.offers` lists only what is sold here and what a search has turned up; never hint at what it does not list.
- `act` reread: a garbled clue is clean now; present its text from `run_view`.
- `question` (the outcome's `kind` is `interrogate`): `succeeded`, `stance` (`gives-ground`: a lie fell; `holds`: they kept to their story), `alibi` (what the suspect says; voice it in full, in the scene described in `talks.md`), `claimed` (only when present: what the suspect claimed in this talk, at the first one their whole story and later the claim of a lie that follows one that fell; have the suspect say each of them aloud, plainly and in their own voice, in the order given, as ordinary talk, and never hint which are true or which are lies, nor that any is doubtful; the claims stay in `suspects[].claims` of the view, and one with `broken` set is a claim that fell), `presented` with `effect` per shown clue (and, when the case gives the suspect a line for it, `reaction`: what the suspect says about that clue, in their own words; play it as spoken, in the scene, and do not explain it or say whether it is true. A clue that did something and has no `reaction` carries a `manner` (`deflect`, `dig-in` or `go-quiet`): play the moment that way, as `talks.md` says, and never leave it as a blank), and `presentedInsights` the same for each conclusion put to them (a conclusion has no `reaction`, only a `manner`) (`breaks-lie`: a lie fell; `confirms`: it contradicts a lie that had already fallen; `implicates`: it is about them but breaks no lie they still hold; `no-effect`: it is not about them, which reads the same as a clue that was brushed off, so show no tell; see `talks.md`), `mood` (how the talks have left the suspect: `calm`, `shaken`, `cornered`, `broken`) and `closed` (set when a pressure with nothing behind it has shut the suspect for a while), `conceded` (the lies that fell, with the suspect's own `statement` and, when present, their `admission`: voice the crack, then let the suspect say the admission in their own words, as the new thing the hero has learned, and write it into the scene as spoken, not summarised), `secret` (only a perfect roll: let it slip), `reputationChange`. Never imply that a suspect still holds lies you were not told about. `told` (only when present) lists clues the suspect let slip in this talk, which are now found: present each from `run_view` text as something said aloud, in the suspect's voice, never as something found on a shelf.
- `deduce`: `result` is `insight` (new; give the insight text from `insights` of the result; when the outcome has `clears`, the conclusion has cleared that suspect: say plainly, in the hero's own thoughts, that this person could not have done it and the hero can cross them off, and from then on play them as someone who has been cleared, relieved or still sore about being suspected, never as a suspect; `cleared` of that suspect in the view stays true), `already-known`, or `nothing` (the link leads nowhere; say so in one wry line about the hero's failed theory, without hinting how close it was and without suggesting other clues to try).
- `accuse`: `verdict`, `culpritRight`, `motiveRight`, `evidenceComplete`, `truth`. In an abduction also `placeRight` (whether the victim was where the hero said) and, in `truth`, `whereaboutsId`, the place the victim was kept; the culprit and the place must both be right for `proven` or `unproven`, and either wrong is `wrong` (tell which was right and which was not, from `culpritRight` and `placeRight`). In a homicide (an attempt) the accusation also names what it was done with, and the outcome has `meansRight` (whether the means the hero named is the one of the case) and, in `truth`, `homicide` (the `victim` and the `fate`, `attempt` for one who lives): a right culprit with the wrong means, no means, or a means never drawn is `unproven` (the `shortfall` then has `means`, with `held`, `offered` and `drawn` as the motive has), and another culprit is `wrong` whatever the means. `run_view` has `victim` (the `name` and whether they are `alive`), public from the first scene, which the board shows as a card above the suspects. Only here does the truth exist for you. `proven`: the case holds. `unproven`: the culprit is right but the motive or evidence did not hold up; the outcome then has a `shortfall` that says what was lacking: `motive` (`held`; `offered`: whether the player put a conclusion forward as the motive; `drawn`: whether the conclusion that explains the motive had ever been drawn) and `evidence` (`held`; `clues`: the clues of the chain of proof that came nearest, each with its `state`, `laid-out`, `found` (found but left out of the proof) or `never-found`, its `description` and, for one found, its `number`). Work it into the closing scene as what the hero understands too late: say plainly which part fell short, the motive, the evidence or both, name each missing clue by its `number` if it was found and by what it was if it never was (the run is over, so it may be told), and say how the hero might have found or used it. Add nothing the `shortfall` does not hold, and do not blame the player. `wrong`: the wrong person, with no `shortfall`. Every verdict also has an `epilogue`, what the closing story is told from (see `endings.md`, "The closing story of an accusation"): `course` (the true course of the crime in order: `atMinute` since the start of the crime day, `actorId`, `locationId`, `action`), `missed` (for `unproven`: each clue of the chain that was never found, with its `description`, its `locationId` and, when a thing in the place held it, the `partId` and the `obstacle` that stood in the way) and `misled` (for `wrong`: the false clues the hero found that pointed at the person they named, each with a `number` and a `description`).
- `run_give_up`: the run ends; a brief, gentle epilogue, no truth unless the result carries it.

## The signal at work

`felt` lists what the hero's character signal did in this turn, and is empty for a hero without a signal and for a turn it did not touch. Each entry names the `signal` and its `effect`: `helped` (the signal made a check of the `skill` easier: the gift worked), `hindered` (it made it harder: the cost showed) or `shielded` (it spared a `meter`, such as the nerves, a loss: the gift worked). Work it into the scene in a clause or a sentence, as the hero's own thought, in the signal's `voice` from the view, and never as a number or a skill name: in love, "the warmth in my head makes the talk go easier" («в голове тепло, и разговор идёт легче»); low, "the dull edge of the day keeps the detail from me" («тусклый день не пускает ко мне детали»); burnt out, "a blunder that would have shaken another leaves me as flat as before" («промах, который выбил бы другого, оставляет меня таким же ровным»). Say it only when it is in `felt`, and add no fact about the case: it colours the hero, never the clues.

## A trait shows itself

`revealed` lists the hidden traits of the hero that showed themselves in this turn, and is empty on most turns. Each entry has the `trait` id, its `polarity` (`positive`, `negative` or `mixed`) and the `reason`: `modifier` (it made a roll easier or harder), `auto-discover` (a second look found a clue without a roll), `lock-out` (a source of information stayed shut to the hero) or `spawn-event` (a debt collector turned up and cost an hour). Play it as a moment in the scene, in a sentence or two, and let the hero notice something about themselves for the first time, in the first person: "I have always read a room faster than a ledger" («я всегда читал людей быстрее, чем бумаги»), "the old grudge at the precinct means nobody there will take my call" («старая обида в участке значит, что мне там не ответят»). After that the trait is open: `run_view` lists it in `revealedTraits` with its effects, and you may name it and use it in the story from then on. Say it only when it is in `revealed`, once, and never hint at a trait that has not been revealed: the count of the hidden ones is the only thing the player may know about them. A trait may show itself in a place whose description does not hint at it: the engine gives each of the hero's traits a place to act in, and the case never knows. Tell the moment as the hero's own, and fit it to the place without contradicting it: a crowd in a hallway, a tight spot behind a shelf, a stranger's ledger left open on a desk, a screen with a login the hero cannot read. Never explain why the place felt so, and never invent a person, a clue or a fact of the case to carry it.

## Events of the turn

`events` lists the random events that happened during this turn: `description` is what the world did, in the words of the case, and `effect` is its kind. Every day brings at least one, at midday or in the evening (or as the hero turns in early, a rest or a collapse), a night may bring its own, and most turns bring none (an empty list). The engine has already applied the effect (an hour lost, frayed nerves, a closed door, a suspect moved), so tell it as something that happened to the hero on the way: a short scene of two to four sentences in the hero's voice, built on `description`, with the effect shown (the hour gone, the shaken hero) and never as a number. `flavor` is colour with no cost. Add no fact beyond the description, and do not say whether it helps the case.

## Refusals (nothing changed, no time passed)

`invalid-intent` (re-check ids), `run-over`, `unknown-location`, `already-here`, `not-adjacent`, `locked`, `location-closed`, `unknown-suspect`, `suspect-elsewhere`, `suspect-closed` (the suspect will not talk for now: say so as the hero finds the door shut, and turn to something else; ## Signal events and fatigue

The `kind` of a signal event is the mood and the cause, nothing more: `partner-message-warm` is a kind word from the hero's partner, `partner-message-cold` a harsh one, `caught-a-cold` a sniffle that lasts to the night, `letter-from-home-warm` a kind letter from family and `letter-from-home-wistful` one that stirs the homesickness. Write the scene yourself. When the event in `run_view`'s `signalEvents` has a `companion`, that is the first name of the partner or the family member: use exactly that name, never another and never a surname, and never contradict the engine's effect on nerves.

## The road

`run_view` has `weather` (an id: `mild`, `warm`, `hot`, `humid`, `windy`, `fog`, `dry-heat`, `cold-rain`, `snow`, `frost`, `storms`) for today, or `null` when the case is set nowhere. It colours every place out of doors that day (the `location.exposure` of the view is `outdoor`; `indoor` is a room in a building, where the weather is only what comes through the window) and the walk between places; say it in a clause the hero would think, never as the id. A walk is slower in bad weather: the `minutes` of the move result already include it, so do not add anything up.

A `move` result may carry `weather` (the walk was out of doors), `hazard` and `roadMishap`, a small, comic thing that happened on the way. Tell it as a short beat of the hero's own thoughts after the arrival, from its id, never harming anyone, and let it cost what the result says (a quarter of an hour more, a point of nerves, a short `exhausted` or `shaken` in the view) and no more:

`hazard` is a trouble of this country's climate (`blizzard`, `ice`, `heavy-rain`, `heatwave`, `drought`, `gale`, `fog`, `dust-wind`) that the weather of the day has brought out: the walk is already longer by it, so do not add anything up, and say it in a clause the hero would think (the pavement is glass, the heat is a hand on the neck), never as the id. On such a day the mishap is often its own.

Never turn a mishap into an injury, a fight or a threat, whatever the weather, and never let it hint at the case: it is the street, not the plot.
