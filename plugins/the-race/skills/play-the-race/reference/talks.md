# Talks with suspects

A talk is the scene the player waits for. The engine gives you the facts of it (`outcome` of `question`) and the person's `persona` (from `run_view`); the scene is yours. A single quoted alibi with a line of staging is a failure.

## The floor

- **Four to eight lines of dialogue** between the hero and the suspect, with the hero's questions in indirect speech or a line each, and the suspect answering in their own words. The scene is told by the hero, so what they see, hear and make of the person is their own thought ("he straightens a pen that is already straight, and I file that under habits, not guilt"), never a verdict. Wrap the exchange in a short opening (how the suspect receives the hero) and a closing beat (how it ends, what the hero is left with). A cold, rushed talk may be the shorter end of the range; a talk that broke a lie or won a secret, the longer.
- **The suspect talks like their persona.** Take the manner from `persona` and keep it for the whole talk: a pedant corrects the hero's wording, a flatterer deflects with compliments, a bureaucrat answers with a form number, a nervous one repeats the question. Give them tics, a topic they love and a way of avoiding the point. The hero may be wry about the manners, never cruel.
- **The approach shapes the scene.** `social`: the suspect is wooed, flattered, offered a chair, and answers with warmth or with slippery charm. `pressure`: the hero leans, the suspect bristles, stalls or goes cold. A failed approach shows in the suspect's reaction (offence, a chill, a joke at the hero's expense), never as a hint about guilt.

## What the engine fixes, and what you may add

The facts are the engine's; the voice around them is yours.

## A talk that gives no clue

When the hero gets nothing, the scene is pure character: the suspect wanders to a pet subject, answers a question with a question, complains about the office, orders tea, stonewalls with style. This filler must carry **no fact of the case**: no new place, time, name, object, motive or relationship, no remark about another suspect, nothing that could later be taken for a clue or contradict the case. Ordinary colour that the case cannot contradict (the weather, the furniture seen in the room, the suspect's habits and opinions) is fine. If you are not sure that a detail is harmless, leave it out.

The hero reads the person through their own trade (a nurse sees the hands, an accountant the numbers they avoid) but only ever as manner, never as proof. Never rank or hint at guilt through the talk, in the suspect's lines or the hero's thoughts. A calm suspect is not more innocent than a fidgety one: give both the same chance to look either way.

## Language

Write the speech in the player's language, in the register of the persona. In Russian use natural spoken forms, not bureaucratic Russian, unless the persona is a bureaucrat. Quotes, em dashes and the suspect's own tics are fine; do not put the suspect's lines in a table, list or labelled "Alibi:".
