# Garbled readings: examples

The engine marks a reading with a `garbleLevel` of 0 to 3. You write the garbled version of a clue's text from the clean text in the view. Rules: keep the clue's gist recognisable, degrade the details, never add a new fact, never "fix" the result into a different clue. The hero does not know their reading is off, so the narration does not announce errors; a short physical cue (rubbed eyes, a fourth coffee) is enough.

Clean text: "A heavy iron key lies under a folding chair, tagged 'Archive' in the curator's tidy handwriting."

- Level 1 (slips): "A heavy iron key lies under a folding chair, tagged 'Archives' in the curator's tidy handwriting, or perhaps the caterer's."
- Level 2 (muddle): "A key, iron or brass, was under a chair or a stool. A tag with some word on it, the curator's hand, or maybe not."
- Level 3 (fog): "Something small and metal on the floor near seats. A tag. Someone's writing. It meant something an hour ago."

Russian, clean: "Под складным стулом лежит тяжёлый железный ключ с биркой «Архив» ровным почерком куратора."

- Уровень 1: "Под складным стулом лежит тяжёлый ключ с биркой «Архивы» ровным почерком куратора, кажется."
- Уровень 2: "Ключ, то ли железный, то ли латунный, под стулом или скамьёй. Бирка со словом, почерк чей-то."
- Уровень 3: "Что-то металлическое у стульев. Бирка. Чей-то почерк. Час назад это что-то значило."

After a `reread`, present the clean text exactly as the view gives it.

Mishap beats, each one or two lines: a stumble over a rolled carpet; coffee down a clean shirt; keys hunted through three pockets; calling the suspect by the wrong name and getting a cold look.
